Calendar and seasonal strips

Contract months

  • Prices are daily closes of the front-month futures contract. When a contract expires the series rolls to the next month, which can cause a step in the line.
  • Brent, TTF and NBP are the NYMEX contracts that settle against ICE Brent, ICE Endex TTF and ICE NBP; they track the ICE benchmarks closely but are not ICE's official settlement prices.
  • Unit conversion: 1 MWh = 3.412 MMBtu = 34.12 therm. Currencies are converted with the EUR/USD, GBP/USD and CNY/USD rate of the same day (or the latest day before it).
  • Forward curves are the daily settlement prices of each NYMEX contract month (DCE for coking coal and coke). Yahoo Finance lists only some of the later months (Brent in particular), and contracts that have expired drop out, so older curves start later. Gaps in a curve are bridged with a straight line; strips are shown only when every month in them has a price. The dotted history line is the monthly average of the front-month price from the Crude oil and Natural gas tabs; the flat bars are the actual yearly averages for past years and the Cal strips for the years ahead.
  • No forward curve is shown for TTF and NBP: the exchanges that host them (ICE Endex, EEX) license their settlement prices and do not allow automated republication.
  • Spot prices marked "EIA" are the US Energy Information Administration's daily spot assessments. They appear once a week, a few days after the trading day.
  • Coal comes in two kinds. Thermal (steam) coal is burnt in power plants; metallurgical (coking) coal is baked into coke, which reduces iron ore to iron in blast furnaces. The daily European and international thermal coal benchmarks (API2 Rotterdam, API4 Richards Bay, Newcastle globalCOAL) and the seaborne coking coal assessments (Australian premium hard coking coal) are licensed by Argus, McCloskey and Platts, and the Chinese thermal coal futures on the Zhengzhou exchange are no longer actively traded, so thermal coal is shown as the IMF's monthly average of the Australian (Newcastle, 6,000 kcal/kg FOB) export price, drawn as a flat step for each month and published one to two months later.
  • Coking coal and coke are the daily settlement prices of the Dalian Commodity Exchange (DCE) main contracts, for delivery in China and incl. 13% VAT; use "excl. VAT" to compare them with the export price. About 1.3–1.4 t of coking coal makes 1 t of coke. Their forward curves are on the Forward curves tab.
  • LNG: the Asian benchmark JKM (S&P Global Platts) and the CME futures on it are licensed, and ACER's daily EU LNG assessment does not allow automated downloads, so LNG is shown as two free monthly prices. "LNG Asia" is the IMF's series for Japan's average LNG import price, which is mostly long-term contracts linked to oil prices, so it follows Brent with a delay rather than the spot market. "US LNG exports" is the EIA's average price of US LNG at the export terminal (before shipping), converted from USD per thousand cubic feet at 1.037 MMBtu/Mcf. Both appear one to a few months after the month ends and are drawn as a flat step for each month.

Futures prices via Yahoo Finance; when Yahoo is unavailable, WTI and Henry Hub front-month settlements come from the EIA Open Data API, which also provides the spot prices. Monthly Asian LNG and Australian thermal coal prices from the IMF Primary Commodity Prices via FRED (LNG, coal). Dalian coking coal and coke futures via Sina Finance.
