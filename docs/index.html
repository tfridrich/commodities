How the figures are calculated

For every delivery day a linear program schedules a 1 MW battery with 1, 2 or 4 MWh of storage, knowing all of that day's prices in advance. The results are therefore an upper bound of what a well-run battery could have earned; real operators earn less because they trade on forecasts and do not win every balancing bid. The "with capture rates" switch scales each revenue part down to a realistic share.

Strategies

  • Day-ahead only: arbitrage on the SDAC day-ahead auction (hourly products until September 2025, 15-minute products since 1 October 2025).
  • FCR only and aFRR only: the battery offers as much FCR, or as much aFRR up and down capacity, as its power and energy allow, at the published average capacity price.
  • Optimised stack: FCR, aFRR up and aFRR down are chosen per 4-hour block together with day-ahead trading. The remaining power and state of charge are used for arbitrage, and enough energy is held back to deliver the reserved capacity.
  • Intraday (Germany, shown when available): after the day-ahead and balancing positions are fixed, the schedule is re-traded against the quarter-hourly ID-AEP index from Netztransparenz, a price index of continuous intraday trading. Austria has no published intraday price, so its intraday revenues are not estimated.

Battery assumptions

    What is not included

    • aFRR activation (energy) revenues: they depend on activation volumes per bid, which are not published per unit. The battery is assumed to be energy-neutral during activations.
    • Market depth: every bid is assumed to be accepted at the average capacity price, as if the battery were too small to move the price. The German aFRR market procures roughly 2 GW per direction and the Austrian one far less, so a growing battery fleet pushes these prices down.
    • Grid fees, balancing group costs, marketing (route-to-market) fees, degradation beyond a small wear cost of 1 €/MWh, and outages.
    • Before 2024 only the day-ahead strategy is shown, because the balancing price history on this site starts in January 2024.
