Metals prices

There is no exchange or daily price for cement or concrete anywhere: ready-mixed concrete has to be poured within an hour or two of mixing, so it is priced plant by plant and project by project. The official measure is the monthly producer price index of each country.

Contract months

  • COMEX copper and SGX iron ore are daily closes of the continuous nearby futures contract; when it rolls to the next month the line can show a step. The free source for these goes back to 2016 (COMEX copper) and December 2022 (SGX iron ore). SGX iron ore settles on the average of the Platts 62% Fe index over its delivery month, so during that month part of its price is already fixed and it moves less than the daily index. SHFE, DCE and GFEX prices are the daily settlement prices of the exchange's main (most traded) contract, which is usually three to five months out, so they are not the front month.
  • Steel: SHFE rebar (HRB400E) and hot-rolled coil (Q235B) are the daily settlement prices of the Shanghai main contracts, incl. 13% VAT, and the most liquid steel futures worldwide. The European and US daily steel benchmarks (hot-rolled coil assessments by Argus, Platts and CRU, and the LME and CME contracts settled on them) are licensed and are not shown. The US indices are producer price indices of the Bureau of Labor Statistics (via FRED); the German index is Eurostat's producer price index for basic metals, which also contains aluminium, copper and other non-ferrous metals. A German index for steel alone is published by Destatis only.
  • Cement and concrete: producer price indices for the US (Bureau of Labor Statistics, via FRED) and Germany (Eurostat). All indices are rebased to their 2021 average = 100 so they can be compared; the "1 year" change is against the same month a year earlier.
  • Gold and silver: COMEX prices are daily closes of the continuous nearby futures in USD per troy ounce (31.1035 g). SHFE gold is quoted in CNY per gram and carries no VAT, because gold traded on a Chinese exchange is exempt; SHFE silver is quoted in CNY per kg and includes 13% VAT like the other Chinese contracts. The "Shanghai premium" spread shows how far Chinese prices sit above (or below) COMEX in the chosen unit; for silver, switch to "excl. VAT" to compare like with like. The London benchmarks (LBMA Gold and Silver Price) are licensed by ICE Benchmark Administration and the LBMA and are not shown.
  • Prices on the Chinese exchanges (SHFE, DCE, GFEX) include 13% VAT (except SHFE gold) and are for delivery in China. Use "excl. VAT" to compare them with the US-listed contracts and the LME. Even without VAT, SHFE copper and COMEX copper differ by import costs and regional premiums, and DCE iron ore is priced per wet tonne at a Chinese port while the SGX contract is per dry tonne delivered (CFR) to China (moisture is typically 8–10%).
  • Daily LME prices are licensed and may not be republished, so the LME appears as the IMF's monthly averages of the LME spot price (copper grade A cathode; aluminium 99.5% minimum purity). The IMF publishes them one to two months after the month ends. On the daily and weekly views each month's average is drawn as a flat step. SHFE aluminium is the daily settlement price of the Shanghai main contract (Al 99.70%). There is no free source for the CME aluminium contracts that can be read automatically, and they trade thinly.
  • There is no free daily source for lithium prices outside China: the CME and SGX lithium contracts settle against Fastmarkets assessments, which are licensed, and they trade too rarely to give a daily series. GFEX lithium carbonate is the reference contract for China, the largest market.
  • Unit conversion: 1 t = 1,000 kg = 2,204.62 lb; 1 troy ounce = 31.1035 g. Currencies are converted with the ECB euro reference rates (USD and CNY per EUR) of the same day, or the latest day before it.
  • Forward curves are the daily settlement prices of each contract month. They are available for the Chinese exchanges, which list twelve consecutive months; there is no free source for the COMEX and SGX contract months that can be read automatically. Gaps in a curve are bridged with a straight line; strips are shown only when every month in them has a price. The dotted history line is the monthly average of the exchange's price on the metal's own tab.
